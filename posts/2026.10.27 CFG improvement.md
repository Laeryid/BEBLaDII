What I love about working on AI is that genuine feeling of being on the cutting edge of science.

Just about a month ago, the paper *Specificity-Aware Diffusion Steering via Variance-Reduced Sequential Monte Carlo* was published (https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.00395).

The authors propose a set of techniques to improve diffusion model generation quality — a topic directly relevant to my current work. While some of the proposed methods are computationally too expensive, one idea turned out to be readily applicable: a dynamic CFG scale.

For those not deeply familiar with the concept, Classifier-Free Guidance (CFG) is a technique used to amplify a useful steering signal — in my case, the influence of the prompt. We compute the model's output both with and without the prompt, getting a directional vector that shifts the prediction towards the desired target. CFG scales this shift up even further, as diffusion models typically don't hit the target spot-on, but merely approximate it.

Drawing on the paper's insights, I tried computing this guidance scale dynamically based on how strongly the prompt affects the response. If the model already considers a token well-aligned with the prompt, there is no need to push the prompt's influence any further — doing so might actually knock the token out of the valid representation manifold.

The practical implementation is much simpler than the paper itself, but the beauty is that I now have a solid mathematical foundation backing this heuristic.
